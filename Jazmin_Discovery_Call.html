Jazmin Flores — Discovery Call

480-307-7627 · Before sending the lease proposal

Goal of this call: find out what her business actually is and whether her numbers support a lease. Do not quote the rate. Do not negotiate. Listen.
Open Familiar tone, neutral frame
"Jazmin? Hey, it's Scott. Before I send the lease proposal over, I want to make sure I build it around what you're actually doing, so it works for both of us. Walk me through your business, what do you have going on?"
Listen for: does "rental" come out on its own? Let her talk until she stops.
Q1 The question that settles everything
"You mentioned you've got events booked the whole stretch. Tell me about those, are these events you're running, or customers renting the trailer from you?"
Neutral wording. Both answers sound fine. No accusation.
Listen for: "renting from me" = re-rental terms, insurance and rate change. "Events I run" = original terms hold.
Q2 Why lease instead of buy
"What made you want to lease instead of buying your own unit?"
You think you know. Let her say it.
Listen for: is she straight about credit? Is this a 7-month bridge to buying her own, or longer?
Q3 Her pricing — the viability check
"What are you charging per event right now?"
Listen for: her rate × her volume must clear the lease with room to spare. Under $1,000/event = thin. $1,200+ = workable.
Q4 Confirmed bookings, not claims
"How many bookings do you have actually confirmed for October through April, deposits in hand?"
Her confirmed book is her real credit report. She doesn't have another one.
Listen for: a number, with deposits. "Booked the entire time" without deposits is a claim, not a book.
Q5 Operations reality
"Where would the trailer live between bookings, and who's doing your pumping and cleaning?"
Listen for: named vendors and a real location = real operation. Vague answers = she's earlier-stage than she sounds.
If it comes out She says she's a rental company
"Good to know, that changes how I structure the insurance and the lease. Let me put the proposal together on that basis."
Calm. No ambush. You guessed event planner, she never lied. Nothing to litigate. Rate conversation happens in writing, not on this call.

Do not on this call

  • Quote the new rate
  • Negotiate anything
  • React to what she reveals
  • Mention the event-planner assumption
  • Commit to a start date
After Where her answers lead
Strong book Confirmed bookings, $1,200+ rates, real vendors → send re-rental proposal at $2,800, $8,100 at signing.
Thin book Few deposits, low rates → offer per-event instead: "Let's start per-event and convert to a monthly lease once you're at three bookings a month." Caps your exposure, keeps the relationship.
Doesn't add up Numbers can't clear the lease at any rate that works for you → no deal. You can't price your way into financing her losses.

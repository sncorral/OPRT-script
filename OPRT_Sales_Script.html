Inbound lead · B2C · standard event
Opening Call the lead — demonstrate you read the form
⌄
Familiar tone with a pause — sounds like you know them. No "is this a good time" — it just invites no. Company name is fine; they need to know who's calling.
Scott
"[Name]? — pause — This is Scott with Old Pueblo Restroom Trailers. I saw you're looking at a trailer for your [event type] on [date]. Calling to make sure we get you squared away."
Scott — after they respond
"Perfect. Tell me a little more about what you've got planned — is this at your home, a venue, that kind of thing?"
Past pain Let them sell themselves
⌄
Don't assume they've had a bad experience. Ask — if they have a story they'll do the work for you.
Scott
"Tell me why you're looking into restroom trailers specifically."
Scott — after they respond
Mirror back the specific thing they said, connect it to the outcome, then close with: "...and your guests are still taken care of in a clean, comfortable space." Reference what they actually told you before that line — don't skip straight to the payoff.
💬 "I don't want people in my house"
"Yeah, that's the exact reason people go this route — keeps your house off limits, and your guests are still taken care of in a clean, comfortable space."
💬 "We're on septic"
"Makes sense — a trailer takes that whole variable out of the equation, nothing running through your system, and your guests are still taken care of in a clean, comfortable space."
💬 "I don't want to clean up after people leave"
"Right, that's the piece most people don't think about until after the event — nothing in your house to deal with, and your guests are still taken care of in a clean, comfortable space."
💬 "We only have one bathroom"
"Yeah, one bathroom for a crowd is exactly where the bottleneck happens — this solves that, and your guests are still taken care of in a clean, comfortable space."
Pitch Outcomes only — under 90 seconds
⌄
Sell Maui, not the plane ride. Outcomes, not features. Then stop talking.
Scott
"Here's what we do. We bring a luxury trailer — flushing toilets, running water, AC, real lighting. Your guests walk in and it feels like a real bathroom. It's the one detail nobody notices when it's done right — and the one thing everybody remembers when it's not.

We handle delivery, setup, and pickup. Just tell me where you want it and I'll handle the rest."

Pause. Let it land. Don't fill the silence.

Ask Quote and ask for the booking
⌄
State the price confidently. Don't hedge. Ask what questions they have — don't ask if they want to book.
Scott
"Standard rate is $1,200 — delivery, setup, and pickup all in. What questions do you have before we get that locked in?"
Objections Common stalls and how to handle them
⌄
Don't answer blind. Don't get defensive. Ask a question about the objection that moves them back toward their own result — let them do the work.
💬 "That's more than I expected"
"What were you expecting to spend?"

[They answer]

"Got it. What was it about what we do that made you reach out in the first place?"

[Let their answer land. If still stuck:]

"If ours was the same price as whatever else you've looked at, which would you go with — and why?"

Let their answer land. Don't fill the silence — don't tell them what they already know.

💬 "I need to think about it"
"Of course. What part are you still working through?"

This stall means there's a real objection you haven't found yet. The answer tells you what it actually is — then handle that.

💬 "I need to talk to my partner / fiancé"
"Of course. What part do you think they'd be uncertain about?"

[Work through the proxy objection. Then:]

"What would make that conversation easy? I can send everything over so you've got it in one place."

Different stall than "think about it" — here you're surfacing a real concern being held by someone not on the call. Get to it now or it kills the deal later.

💬 "We found someone cheaper"
"If the prices were the same, which would you go with — and why?"

Let their answer land. They'll tell you why they prefer yours. Don't add to it.

💬 "We're still getting quotes / still looking around"
"What are the main things you're comparing when you look at different options?"

Gets you in front of their decision criteria before they've made the decision. Now you know what matters to them.

💬 "I don't think we need something that nice"
"What are you picturing for your guests when they need to use the restroom during the event?"

Gets them to say out loud what they actually want — then connect what they said to what you deliver.

💬 "We were just going to use the house bathroom"
"Is the house set up to handle that many guests cycling through without a line? Some places are totally fine — others it becomes the thing everyone talks about after. What's your gut feeling?"

Don't argue. Let them think it through themselves.

💬 "We were thinking porta-potties"
"Have you been to an event where that was the setup? What was that like for the guests?"

[If no story:]

"What's your main concern with guests having to use a porta-potty at your event?"

If they have a story, stop — they'll sell themselves. The follow-up question is only for when they don't.

💬 Any hesitation on price
"What is it specifically that's making the price feel tight right now?"

Listen. Don't defend yet. The answer tells you what to address.

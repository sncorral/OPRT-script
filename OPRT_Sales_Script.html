Outdoor event — form submission

Inbound lead · B2C · standard event

Opening Call the lead — demonstrate you read the form
⌄
Never open with "Is this [name]?" — that puts you on defense immediately.
Scott
"[Name]? — pause — This is Scott with Old Pueblo Restroom Trailers. I saw you're looking at a trailer for your [event type] on [date] — [guest count] guests. I just wanted to reach out before that date gets taken. Did I catch you at an okay time?"
Scott — after they respond
"Perfect. Tell me a little more about what you've got planned — is this at your home, a venue, that kind of thing?"
Past pain Let them sell themselves
⌄
Don't assume they've had a bad experience. Ask — if they have a story, they'll tell it and do the work for you.
Scott
"Have you been to an outdoor event where the restroom situation wasn't great — porta-potties, long lines, that kind of thing? What was that like?"
Scott — after they respond
"Yeah. That's exactly what we make sure doesn't happen at your event."
Pitch Outcomes only — under 90 seconds
⌄
Sell Maui, not the plane ride. Outcomes, not features. Then stop talking.
Scott
"Here's what we do. We bring a luxury trailer — flushing toilets, running water, AC, real lighting. Your guests walk in and it feels like part of the venue. Nobody's thinking about the bathroom, which means the only thing they remember is your event.

We handle delivery, setup, and pickup. You make one call. That piece of your day is handled."

Pause. Let it land. Don't fill the silence.

Ask Quote and ask for the booking
⌄
State the price confidently. Don't hedge. Ask what questions they have — don't ask if they want to book.
Scott
"Based on what you've told me, I think we're a great fit for your event. For [guest count] guests we're at $1,200 — delivery, setup, and pickup all in. I can hold your date right now. What questions do you have before we get that locked in?"
Objections Common stalls and how to handle them
⌄
💬 "That's more than I expected"
"Totally fair — what were you expecting to spend?"

[They answer]

"Got it. If ours was the same price as whatever else you've looked at, which would you go with — and why?"

[They tell you why they prefer yours. Then:]

"Right — that's the difference. You already know it."
💬 "I need to think about it" / "I need to talk to my partner"
"Of course. Out of curiosity, what do you think their main concern would be?"

[Work through the real objection, not the stall. Then:]

"What would make it easy to have that conversation? I can send you everything so you've got it in one place."
💬 "We were just going to use the inside bathroom"
"Is the house set up to handle [guest count] guests cycling through one bathroom all night without a line? Some houses are totally fine — others it becomes the thing everyone remembers. What's your gut on it?"

Don't argue. Let them think it through themselves.

💬 "We were thinking porta-potties"
"Have you been to an event where that was the setup? What was it like?"

[If they have a story, they'll sell themselves. If not:]

"The difference for your guests is real — especially in the summer heat. They walk into a porta-potty and they're thinking about getting out. They walk into ours and they forget they're at an outdoor event. That's the version you want."
💬 Any hesitation on price
"What is it specifically that's making the price feel tight right now?"

Listen. Don't defend yet. The answer tells you what to address.

Date hold Offer proactively if they're hesitating
⌄
You have one trailer. A held date that doesn't convert is a real cost. Give them a short window — creates urgency without pressure, and pushes toward the deposit as the real commitment.
Scott
"I can hold your date for 48 hours — no deposit yet. After that I have to open it back up. If you want to lock it in for good, the deposit takes care of that today."

Warm lead who needs to check with a partner → 48-hour hold is fair. Cold lead just window shopping → they won't take the hold seriously anyway. Either way, push toward the deposit as the real lock.

Reinforce After deposit — confirm next steps
⌄
Most salespeople stop here. Don't. Reinforce the decision and set the next step — they should never wonder what happens next.
Scott
"[Name], you made a great call. A lot of people try to cut corners on this and their guests always notice. You made sure they won't.

Here's what happens next — I'll send over the rental agreement and deposit instructions. Once that's in, your date is locked. Any questions before I do that?"

BAMFAM — every call ends with a confirmed next step.
